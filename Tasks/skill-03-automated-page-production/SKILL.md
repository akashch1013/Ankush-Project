# Automated Page Production

Work from the current assignment through a lettered rough, finished panels, an editable InDesign page and its proof. Produce the requested range or stage. Keep a design-only request at the rough and handoff stage. For page creation, carry the chosen design through assembly. Use the book's own reading direction, visual language and editorial conventions.

## 1. Establish what the reader should see

Read [Sources and continuity](references/sources-and-continuity.md). Start in the current project’s Sources, including files already there when the request has no new attachments. Open the assigned manuscript, the supplied design manual and the relevant master location or sub-location images. Use the manuscript to decide what appears and when, the manual to art-direct the page, and the location artwork to establish the setting, colours, materials and illustration style. Record the manuscript passage, applicable manual section or visual example, and chosen location image title and filename in the page brief.

Read the production notes, assigned passage and neighbouring page turns. Inventory printable words, silent actions, speaker changes, inscriptions, mathematical evidence, blanks and reveals. Inspect the original equations, tables and pictures. Preserve assigned page and panel boundaries. For prose or scene breaks only, group the passage into provisional panels and record those choices. Preserve wording, speakers, story order and learning sequence.

Use any supplied cast sheets, map, register and approved preceding page. Take format, checks and approval status from this assignment. Read a new or replaced design manual including its visual examples. Reuse a verified full review when the source fingerprint matches, then reopen the applicable sections. When a source is genuinely absent, work from the available evidence and record any provisional visual choices.

## 2. Choose the setting and stage each story beat

1. Inventory the provided location images, including images inside archives. Read each image's visible title, filename and register entry when available. Open the candidates. Match the actual place, physical side, camera orientation, landmarks and equipment state to the moment. Record the selected file and why it works. Similar titles can show different moments. Resolve a misleading or missing title from visible evidence and the manuscript.
2. Choose the least change that makes the action read. Reuse the view, crop or zoom to a supported detail, or derive a new camera angle from the matched references. Give a reason for a new view. Anchor it to the map and visible landmarks. Keep stage geography, scale and screen direction intelligible. A zoom cannot show a hidden face of an object.
3. Block each visible character into that setting. Give them a place and depth, body direction, weight, pose, expression, gaze and hand or paw action. Specify the surface touched, the prop owner, and the exact speaker and listener. Retain cast proportions, clothing and temporary states. Match the art treatment, lighting direction, scale, perspective, shadows and occlusion to the references. Make the action legible with the lettering hidden.
4. Rough the figures into the scene on the actual source crop, making space for exact captions and balloons. Keep the incoming position and establish the outgoing one. If the image set has a genuine gap, derive a supported view or record the exact visible detail that needs a reference. Continue unaffected panels.

## 3. Size the panels and design the whole page

Work at the intended page proportions. Fit the hardest element first, such as a long line of speech, a contact gesture or a wide table. Choose each panel's width, height, position and crop from its job in the sequence. Give the decisive action or comparison enough area to be read. Use gutters, alignment, negative space and changes of scale to guide the eye. Preserve a repeated camera when the joke, change or explanation depends on it.

Place the real words, balloon shapes and tails on the rough. Read the page in order, including within each panel. Compare the strongest layout with a credible alternative when the design is open or the manual requests it. Fit both to the same text and boundaries. Record the reason for the choice. Adapt a fixed author layout only within the requested scope. Check adjacent pages together. Settle the panel aspect ratios before investing in final artwork.

Draw the page palette from the selected master or sub-location artwork. Carry the same named colours and their tints through the background, gutters, borders and embellishments of story, maths and mixed pages. Choose the scale and placement of any motif using the design manual. Keep the palette consistent through a continuous location or teaching sequence.

For maths, practice, revision and mixed pages, use [Maths-page art direction](references/maths-page-art-direction.md) and the relevant teaching-page and figure examples in the supplied manual. Art-direct the whole rough before illustration. Choose what first draws the learner’s eye, how they move from question to figure to working, and how the setting’s colours, materials and edge treatment appear in the maths graphics and diagrams. Use the relevant location image as a visual reference even on a teaching page with no printed scene. On mixed pages, design the transition between the story action and the mathematics.

## 4. Make and inspect the finished panels

Generate, edit or composite each needed story illustration using the chosen location, the available identity references for every visible character and the preceding scene where relevant. Stage figures on a locked background when physical accuracy depends on it. Brief the scene from foreground to background with the assigned camera, action, expression, prop contact, light and intended crop. Reuse suitable approved art. Keep working titles, callouts and reference arrows off the reader's page. Build exact lettering and mathematical evidence separately.

Work one illustrated panel at a time. Inspect its actual crop both at reading size and close up. Overlay the real lettering immediately. Check the action, eyeline, scale, hand or paw contact, prop state, shadows and clearance. Fix a local defect locally. If placement hides a clue or spoils the reading order, revise the rough and all affected placements before continuing. For a batch, finish and review each page before progressing as far as the available InDesign route permits.

## 5. Hand the design to InDesign

Follow [InDesign placement and export](references/indesign-workflow.md). Produce a placement plan with page coordinates, panel and source IDs, linked assets and crops, layer order, exact editable text and styling, caption and balloon geometry, tail targets, editable maths labels, and vector geometry or artwork. Give each frame a stable name. Include the margins, gutters, bleeds and output profile chosen for this edition.

Use the actual InDesign runner available to the project. The bundled builder turns the same plan into a runnable InDesign script and, for supported assets, a linked editable IDML immediately. Preserve the matching plan and links when handing off to desktop InDesign. When native execution is available, run it, inspect the exported PDF and correct the source. Label an interim reading proof and any pending native checks accurately.

## 6. Proof the page and carry the story forward

Use [Proof and checkpoints](references/review-and-checkpoints.md). Check the reader's route at actual size, word for word, panel by panel. Verify mathematical facts and counts, who speaks or acts, what changes and what the next page can now show. Apply the current project's complete checklist. Validate links, fonts, glyphs, overflow and page geometry in the actual production route. Retain exact source and asset revisions, the chosen plan, review observations, open issues and the outgoing state. Deliver the requested editable page, links and individual or combined proofs. Keep author approval distinct from production review.
