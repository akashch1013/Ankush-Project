# Design a maths or mixed page

## Start with the learner's task

Identify what the reader must recognise, count, compare, calculate, explain or do independently. Read the entire question, any supplied diagram, working, conclusion, aside and later reveal. Preserve the source wording, operation signs, units, grouping, precision, exact values and deliberate blanks. Keep exercises unanswered where assigned. If the maths in the manuscript and a source answer disagree, locate the specific discrepancy and flag it without silently rewriting the teaching.

Choose a page treatment for the actual material. A worked example may need a vertical line of reasoning. A comparison may need aligned columns. A diagram-led question may need a generous figure with its question beside it. Practice needs clear entry points and writing space. Keep a solution on its assigned side of the reveal. Fit the hardest example or widest table at reading size before locking the layout. Where the manuscript assigns a companion aside, place it after its specified teaching unit and give its speaker the right expression. On mixed pages record the reader's route from the story clue through the question and onwards.

## Art-direct the rough from the project sources

Put the manuscript assignment, an applicable visual example in the supplied design manual, and the selected master or sub-location image side by side. Read the image title and inspect the actual artwork. Use the location named in the passage or established by the surrounding story. A maths-only page can carry the look of that place entirely through its diagrams and page design. If no suitable location art is provided, use the manual’s palette or the established sequence design and record that choice.

Write a short, concrete direction for the page. Name the manual section or example, the principle borrowed, what the learner sees first, how their eye reaches the task and where they can work. Choose one feature visible in the location art, such as the profile of a frame, a painted edge or a material finish. Show how it shapes the figure, reading surface, border or corner. Use the manual to decide how prominent it should be. Give a discovery or comparison room to attract the eye. Use quieter edges on a dense practice page.

For example, if the reference shows a painted frame with a metal lip, its profile can shape the edge of a worked-example panel. Its accent colour can trace one movement on the number line. Give that movement its own arrowhead and label, and leave the intervals clear. Find an equivalent detail in the actual reference for each new setting.

## Give diagrams a visual language

Write a brief for each figure. Name its purpose, exact source quantities and geometry, representation, labels and units, visual key, line hierarchy, typography, destination size and incoming or revealed state. Reuse an authoritative supplied diagram accurately when suitable. Build exact axes, tables, grids, fractions, worked notation, geometry, counts and labels in native objects or a linked, checked vector with editable source. Reserve image generation for illustrative surroundings and qualitative context. For complex notation use a verified equation workflow and keep its editable source with the page.

Translate the selected location’s art style into the kind of graphic the maths needs. An illustrated object can carry the reference’s contours, surface finish and light. A graph or geometry figure can echo its edge treatment and accent colours while keeping axes, intervals, boundaries and labels exact and editable. Use consistent arrows, endpoints, line weights and colour meanings. Distinguish an interval from a tick and a cell from a border. Check dimensions, totals, ordering, labels, scale and whether a diagram leaks an answer. Do the calculation independently and inspect the final diagram itself.

## Choose the background and compose the page

Build a small set of named swatches by sampling representative parts of the selected location artwork. Sample painted surfaces, materials or shadows inside the scene, away from reference titles and callouts. Record the image title and filename, the visible source of each colour and the final colour values. Reuse the same swatches across the page. Derive a lighter tint or deeper shade when the manual or reading contrast calls for it.

| Page element | Art direction |
| --- | --- |
| Page background and reading surfaces | Use a quiet field from the location palette or a pale tint of it under text and working. Carry a stronger local tone into exposed gutters if the manual supports it. |
| Maths graphics, diagrams and tables | Use a strong legible ink and give each accent a specific teaching job, such as tracing movement or comparing quantities. Keep assigned colour keys intact. Pair meaningful colours with a label, hatch, marker or line pattern. |
| Borders and embellishments | Use colours from that same set and a motif visible in the chosen location. Vary its scale and placement according to the manual. Keep ornament outside countable shapes, scales and answer space. |

A scene fragment, graphic border or fair prop can connect teaching to the story when the live material supports it. Ground it in the current setting. Use open space next to an authorised companion purposefully. Keep actual words, workings, data, empty answer cells and counted shapes clear. Judge the treatment from the reader's page, including whether a decorative element could be counted as part of the question.

## Send the maths to assembly

Give each teaching block, equation, vector, figure label, unanswered area and aside a source ID and frame. Assign the chosen swatches to the actual diagram objects, backgrounds and edge details in the InDesign plan and in any linked vector source. Specify layer order, exact quantities, editable construction and label ownership. Distinguish a question-state diagram from its solution-state version. Read the question and attempt it using only what is visible at that point. Compare the proof with the manual and location reference at intended reading size. Check that the emphasis invites the intended first step and that the figure still makes sense in greyscale.
