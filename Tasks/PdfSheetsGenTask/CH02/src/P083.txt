[PanelizationLabel] ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT
The sign √ means “square root”. For √9, ask, “Which non-negative number multiplied by itself gives 9?” Since 3 × 3 = 9, we have √9 = 3.
The sign ∛ means “cube root”. For ∛8, ask, “Which number times itself times itself gives 8?” Since 2 × 2 × 2 = 8, we have ∛8 = 2. The small 3 belongs to the root sign; it is not a number to multiply by.
Some roots, such as √2, have long decimal values. A scientific calculator can find them. Pi, written π, is another number with a long decimal: π = 3.14159… . Use the calculator’s π key so you keep more digits than you would by typing 3.14.

To find √2, clear the previous calculation, choose the square-root function and enter 2. Before pressing equals, check that the screen shows √2. The result begins 1.414213… . The picture shows which digits to keep for 3 decimal places.
For a cube root such as ∛9, choose the cube-root function and enter 9. If your calculator uses a root template, put 3 in the small index position. Check that the display shows ∛9. The order of key presses can differ between calculators, so check the expression on the screen.
The small 2 in π² means π × π. Use the π key and the square function; do not first replace π with 3.14.
We can check a root’s size without all its decimal digits. Since 1 × 1 = 1 and 2 × 2 = 4, and 1 < 2 < 4, the number √2 must lie between 1 and 2.
