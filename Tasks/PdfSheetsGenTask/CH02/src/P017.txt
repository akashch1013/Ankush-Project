Example 2
[Question] Arrange 3/2, 6/5 and 13/10 in ascending order.
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] We want the smallest fraction first. Write each fraction as a mixed number:
[WorkedStep] 3/2 = 2/2 + 1/2 = 1 1/2.
[WorkedStep] 6/5 = 5/5 + 1/5 = 1 1/5.
[WorkedStep] 13/10 = 10/10 + 3/10 = 1 3/10.
[WorkedStep] The whole-number part is 1 in all three. Compare the fraction parts: 1/2, 1/5 and 3/10. Use denominator 10, because 2 × 5 = 10 and 5 × 2 = 10.
[WorkedStep] 1/2 = (1 × 5)/(2 × 5) = 5/10.
[WorkedStep] 1/5 = (1 × 2)/(5 × 2) = 2/10. The third fraction, 3/10, already has denominator 10.
[WorkedStep] Compare the fraction parts: 2/10 < 3/10 < 5/10.
[WorkedStep] So 1 2/10 < 1 3/10 < 1 5/10. In the original fractions, the ascending order is 6/5, 13/10, 3/2.
Your turn after Example 2
[Question] Arrange 5/2, 21/10 and 12/5 in ascending order.
Check your method
[WorkedStep] Write each fraction as a mixed number: 5/2 = 2 1/2, 21/10 = 2 1/10, and 12/5 = 2 2/5.
[WorkedStep] All three have a whole-number part of 2. Compare 1/2, 1/10 and 2/5 by using denominator 10:
[WorkedStep] 1/2 = (1 × 5)/(2 × 5) = 5/10; 2/5 = (2 × 2)/(5 × 2) = 4/10.
[WorkedStep] Since 1/10 < 4/10 < 5/10, the mixed numbers are 2 1/10 < 2 4/10 < 2 5/10.
[WorkedStep] Smallest to greatest, in the original fractions: 21/10, 12/5, 5/2.
[WorkedStep] In that question, all the whole-number parts were 2. The next question has different whole-number parts. Check those before comparing the fractions.
