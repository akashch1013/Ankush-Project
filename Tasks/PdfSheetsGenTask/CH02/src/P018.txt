Example 3
[Question] Arrange 7/3, 5/4 and 15/8 in ascending order.
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] First write each fraction as a mixed number. Divide the top number by the bottom number:
[WorkedStep] 7 ÷ 3 = 2 remainder 1, so 7/3 = 2 1/3.
[WorkedStep] 5 ÷ 4 = 1 remainder 1, so 5/4 = 1 1/4.
[WorkedStep] 15 ÷ 8 = 1 remainder 7, so 15/8 = 1 7/8.
[WorkedStep] The number 2 1/3 is greater than 2. Both 1 1/4 and 1 7/8 are less than 2. So 7/3 is the greatest fraction.
[WorkedStep] Now compare 1 1/4 and 1 7/8. Their whole-number parts are both 1. Change 1/4 to eighths: 1/4 = (1 × 2)/(4 × 2) = 2/8.
[WorkedStep] Since 2/8 < 7/8, we have 1 1/4 < 1 7/8.
[WorkedStep] Ascending order: 5/4, 15/8, 7/3. Check: 1 1/4 < 1 7/8 < 2 1/3.

[Question] Arrange 19/9, 21/18 and 16/5 in ascending order.
Check your method
[WorkedStep] 19 ÷ 9 = 2 remainder 1, so 19/9 = 2 1/9.
[WorkedStep] 21 ÷ 18 = 1 remainder 3, so 21/18 = 1 3/18.
[WorkedStep] Let’s simplify 3/18: divide both numbers by 3. Then 3/18 = (3 ÷ 3)/(18 ÷ 3) = 1/6. We call this simplifying because we use smaller numbers for the same amount. So 1 3/18 = 1 1/6.
[WorkedStep] 16 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 1, so 16/5 = 3 1/5.
[WorkedStep] The whole-number parts are 2, 1 and 3. Put the number beginning with 1 first, then the number beginning with 2, then the number beginning with 3: 1 1/6 < 2 1/9 < 3 1/5.
[WorkedStep] Ascending order: 21/18, 19/9, 16/5.
Descending order means greatest first, then the next smaller, then the smallest. Let’s use that order in Example 4.
