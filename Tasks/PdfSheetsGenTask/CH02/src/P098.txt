## Multiplying and Dividing by Powers of 10
Multiplying by 100 is the same as multiplying by 10 twice, because 100 = 10 × 10. We write 10 × 10 as 10².
In 10², the small raised 2 tells us to multiply two 10s. It is called an index; the plural is indices. So 10² means 10 × 10 = 100, not 10 × 2 = 20.
Read the next three examples by counting the factors of 10.
10² = 10 × 10 = 100
10³ = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000
10⁴ = 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 10 000

Look at the first place-value chart, for 4.51 × 100. Each digit becomes worth 100 times as much: 4 ones become 4 hundreds = 400; 5 tenths become 5 tens = 50; 1 hundredth becomes 1 one = 1. Add: 400 + 50 + 1 = 451.
So 4.51 × 100 = 451. Each digit moves 2 places left in the chart. The red decimal-point line stays between ones and tenths.
For 0.028 × 1000, 2 hundredths become 2 tens = 20, and 8 thousandths become 8 ones = 8. So 0.028 × 1000 = 20 + 8 = 28. Each digit moves 3 places left.
Division goes the other way. For 8.5 ÷ 100, 8 ones become 8 hundredths = 0.08, and 5 tenths become 5 thousandths = 0.005. So 8.5 ÷ 100 = 0.08 + 0.005 = 0.085. Each digit moves 2 places right.
For 137 ÷ 1000, each digit moves 3 places right in the chart: 137 ÷ 1000 = 0.137. The 1 now means one tenth, the 3 means three hundredths and the 7 means seven thousandths.
Use zeros to show empty places. In 0.085, the 0 after the decimal point shows there are no tenths. Leaving it out would give 0.85, a different number.
