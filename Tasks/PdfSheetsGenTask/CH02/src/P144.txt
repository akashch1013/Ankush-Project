[PanelizationLabel] ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT
The two quantities in our fraction must use the same unit. For “9 cents as a percentage of $1.50”, first change $1.50 to 150 cents. Then compare 9 cents with 150 cents. Let’s do that carefully.
Example 37
[Question] Express each as a percentage.
[Question] (a)  5 as a percentage of 10
[Question] (b)  9 cents as a percentage of $1.50
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a)  The whole we compare with is 10. We have 5 of those 10, so the fraction is 5/10 = 1/2. Half of 100% is 50%, so 5 is 50% of 10.
[WorkedStep] (b)  First match the units. We cannot compare 9 cents with the number 1.50 as though it also meant cents. One dollar is 100 cents, so $1.50 = 100 cents + 50 cents = 150 cents.
[WorkedStep] The fraction is 9/150. Multiply by 100%: 9/150 × 100% = 900/150% = 6%, since 150 × 6 = 900. Check: 1% of 150 cents is 1.5 cents, and 6 × 1.5 cents = 9 cents.
Your turn after Example 37
[Question] Express each as a percentage.
[Question] (a)  8 as a percentage of 20
[Question] (b)  45 cm as a percentage of 1.2 m
Check your method
[WorkedStep] (a)  8/20 × 100% = 8 × (100 ÷ 20)% = 8 × 5% = 40%.
[WorkedStep] (b) We are comparing 45 cm with 1.2 m. First change 1.2 m to centimetres: 1.2 × 100 cm = 120 cm.
[WorkedStep] Now both lengths use centimetres. The fraction is 45/120, so the percentage is 45/120 × 100% = 4500/120% = 37.5%.
