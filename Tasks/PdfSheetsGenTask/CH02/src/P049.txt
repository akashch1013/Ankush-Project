Example 10
[Question] Rina has 9/10 L of cooking oil. She pours it into bottles holding 1/6 L each. How many full bottles can she fill?
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] We have 9/10 L of oil. Each bottle holds 1/6 L. Divide the total oil by the amount for one bottle:
[WorkedStep] Number of bottlefuls = 9/10 ÷ 1/6.
[WorkedStep] 9/10 ÷ 1/6 = 9/10 × 6/1 = 54/10 = (54 ÷ 2)/(10 ÷ 2) = 27/5.
[WorkedStep] Change 27/5 to a mixed number: 27 ÷ 5 = 5 remainder 2, so 27/5 = 5 2/5.
[WorkedStep] This means 5 full bottles and 2/5 of another bottle. The question asks for full bottles, so the answer is 5.
[WorkedStep] Let’s check the oil left. Five bottles use 5 × 1/6 = 5/6 L. Compare that with the 9/10 L available, using denominator 30:
[WorkedStep] 5/6 = (5 × 5)/(6 × 5) = 25/30 L; 9/10 = (9 × 3)/(10 × 3) = 27/30 L.
[WorkedStep] Oil left: 27/30 − 25/30 = 2/30 = 1/15 L. Six full bottles would need 6 × 1/6 = 1 L, more than our 9/10 L. So we must stop at 5 full bottles.
[WorkedStep] In the picture, each rectangle is 1 bottle holding 1/6 L. Five are full. The sixth is 2/5 full. Here 2/5 describes a fraction of a bottle; the actual oil left is 1/15 L, not 2/5 L.

Your turn after Example 10
[Question] Mr Li has 12/13 kg of rice. He packs it into bags holding 3/13 kg each. How many full bags can he fill?
Check your method
[WorkedStep] 12/13 ÷ 3/13 = 12/13 × 13/3 = 12/3 = 4.
[WorkedStep] He fills 4 full bags, with no rice left. Check: 4 × 3/13 = 12/13 kg.
