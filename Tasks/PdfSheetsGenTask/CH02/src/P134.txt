# 2.9 Converting a Fraction or Decimal to a Percentage
We have travelled from percentages to fractions and decimals. Now let’s reverse the journey. If 10 sweets out of 150 are green, the fraction is 10/150. How could we describe that share “out of 100”? We will try it with a collection, then turn the idea into a calculation.
## Knowledge Building Task
Work in a group of five if possible. Use a packet of sweets with five or more colours. Count all the sweets first, then count each colour. Do not eat any until the counts are recorded, or the whole may quietly get smaller!
Open a spreadsheet. Each box is called a cell. Its name gives its column and row: F1 means column F, row 1. Put the total number of sweets in F1. Put the colour names across row 2, starting at B2, and each colour’s count directly below it in row 3.
The sample uses five colours. If your packet has more, continue the colour columns to the right. Keep the total in F1; every sweet must appear in exactly one colour count.
