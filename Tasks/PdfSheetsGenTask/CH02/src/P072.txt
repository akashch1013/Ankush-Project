Example 14
[Question] Which is greater in each pair?
[Question] (a)  0.6 and 4/5
[Question] (b)  3.18 and 16/5
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a)  Put both numbers in decimal form. To change fifths to tenths, 4/5 = (4 × 2)/(5 × 2) = 8/10 = 0.8. Both whole parts are zero. At the tenths place, 6 < 8, so 0.6 < 4/5.
[Question] Think: change both 0.6 and 4/5 to fractions. Which is greater? Does this agree with the decimal method?
Check your method
[WorkedStep] 0.6 = 6/10 = (6 ÷ 2)/(10 ÷ 2) = 3/5. Since 3/5 < 4/5, the greater number is 4/5. Both methods agree.
[WorkedStep] (b)  16/5 = (16 × 2)/(5 × 2) = 32/10 = 3.2. Write 3.2 as 3.20 to line up the places. Both numbers have 3 wholes. Compare their tenths next: 1 < 2, so 3.18 < 3.20. Therefore 3.18 < 16/5.
[WorkedStep] A longer decimal is not automatically bigger. Compare from the left, place by place; stop at the first difference.
Your turn after Example 14
[Question] Which is greater, 1.92 or 1 1/2?
Check your method
[WorkedStep] Since 1/2 = 0.5, we have 1 1/2 = 1.50. Compare 1.92 with 1.50: the whole parts match, but 9 tenths is more than 5 tenths. The greater number is 1.92.
