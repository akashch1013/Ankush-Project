[WorkedStep] For a group of equal items, we can also find one item’s percentage first. Let’s compare that route with the fraction method.
Example 38
[Question] In a class of 25 students, 10 wear spectacles. Find each percentage using two methods, and compare the methods.
[Question] (a)  Students who wear spectacles
[Question] (b)  Students who do not wear spectacles
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a)  Method 1 uses a fraction of the class. Ten of the 25 students wear spectacles, so 10/25 × 100% = 10 × (100 ÷ 25)% = 10 × 4% = 40%.
[WorkedStep] Method 2 finds one student's share first. All 25 students represent 100%. Divide equally: 100% ÷ 25 = 4% per student. Then 10 students represent 10 × 4% = 40%.
[WorkedStep] (b) Method 1: first count the students without spectacles. There are 25 − 10 = 15. Their percentage is 15/25 × 100% = 15 × (100 ÷ 25)% = 15 × 4% = 60%.
[WorkedStep] Method 2: the whole class is 100%, and 40% wear spectacles. So the percentage without spectacles is 100% − 40% = 60%. This is shorter once we know 40%.
[WorkedStep] Both methods give 60%. Check: 40% + 60% = 100%, so the two groups include all 25 students.
Your turn after Example 38
[Question] In a class of 40 students, 28 belong to the swimming team. Find each percentage using two methods.
[Question] (a)  Students in the swimming team
[Question] (b)  Students not in the swimming team
Check your method
[WorkedStep] (a) Method 1: compare the 28 team members with all 40 students: 28/40 × 100% = 2800/40% = 70%.
[WorkedStep] Method 2: 1 student represents 100% ÷ 40 = 2.5%. So 28 students represent 28 × 2.5% = 70%.
[WorkedStep] (b) Method 1: 40 − 28 = 12 students are not in the team. Their percentage is 12/40 × 100% = 1200/40% = 30%.
[WorkedStep] Method 2: subtract the team’s percentage from 100%: 100% − 70% = 30%. Check: 70% + 30% = 100%.
