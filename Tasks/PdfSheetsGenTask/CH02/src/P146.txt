Example 39
[Question] A basket contains 20 fruits. Eight are apples and the rest are oranges.
[Question] (a)  What percentage are apples?
[Question] (b)  What percentage are oranges?
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) The basket contains 20 fruits in all, so use 20 underneath. The 8 apples represent 8/20 × 100% = 8 × (100 ÷ 20)% = 8 × 5% = 40%.
[WorkedStep] (b) Count the oranges: 20 − 8 = 12. Their percentage is 12/20 × 100% = 12 × 5% = 60%.
[WorkedStep] Or subtract the apples’ share from the whole basket: 100% − 40% = 60%. This works because every fruit is either an apple or an orange.
Your turn after Example 39
[Question] A shopping mall has 60 shops. Of these, 45 are eateries and the rest are non-eateries.
[Question] (a)  What percentage are eateries?
[Question] (b)  What percentage are non-eateries?
Check your method
[WorkedStep] (a) Eateries: 45/60 × 100% = (45 ÷ 15)/(60 ÷ 15) × 100% = 3/4 × 100% = 75%.
[WorkedStep] (b) There are 60 − 45 = 15 non-eateries. Their percentage is 15/60 × 100% = 1/4 × 100% = 25%. Check: 100% − 75% = 25%.
