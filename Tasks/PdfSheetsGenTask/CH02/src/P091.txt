[PanelizationLabel] ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT
For subtraction, line up the decimal points and work from right to left. If a top digit is too small to subtract from, borrow from the place on its left. We’ll show the changed digits above the calculation.
Example 21
[Question] Estimate without a calculator. Then evaluate.
[Question] (a)  30.92 − 10.11
[Question] (b)  17.625 − 2.3 − 14.162
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) For 30.92 − 10.11, estimate using 31 and 10: 31 − 10 = 21.
[WorkedStep] For the exact calculation, write 30.92 above 10.11 and line up their decimal points.

[WorkedStep] Subtract from right to left: hundredths 2 − 1 = 1; tenths 9 − 1 = 8; whole-number parts 30 − 10 = 20.
[WorkedStep] 30.92 − 10.11 = 20.81. Check: 20.81 + 10.11 = 30.92.
[WorkedStep] (b) We need 17.625 − 2.3 − 14.162. Estimate with 18, 2 and 14: 18 − 2 − 14 = 2.
[WorkedStep] Work from left to right. First line up the places: 17.625 − 2.300 = 15.325.
[WorkedStep] Now subtract 14.162 from 15.325. In the hundredths column, 2 is smaller than 6. Borrow from the 3 next door: the 3 becomes 2, and the 2 becomes 12. One tenth is ten hundredths, so the amount stays the same: 0.32 = 0.20 + 0.12.

[WorkedStep] Start on the right: 5 − 2 = 3. Then use the replacement numbers: 12 − 6 = 6 and 2 − 1 = 1. Finish with 5 − 4 = 1.
[WorkedStep] So 17.625 − 2.3 − 14.162 = 15.325 − 14.162 = 1.163.
[WorkedStep] Check by adding back both amounts taken away: 1.163 + 14.162 = 15.325, and 15.325 + 2.300 = 17.625.
