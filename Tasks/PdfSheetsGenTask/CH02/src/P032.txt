Next we will add 3 1/3 + 1 3/4. The fraction parts have denominators 3 and 4. Neither 3 nor 4 works as a common denominator, so we will find a new denominator for both fractions.
Example 7
[Question] Evaluate each sum.
[Question] (a)  3 1/3 + 1 3/4
[Question] (b)  4 1/5 + 1 1/3
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) First estimate using nearby halves: 3 1/3 is near 3 1/2, and 1 3/4 is near 1 1/2. So a useful estimate is 3 1/2 + 1 1/2 = 5.
[WorkedStep] Now add the whole-number parts exactly: 3 + 1 = 4.
[WorkedStep] For the fraction parts, 1/3 and 3/4, find a common denominator. Multiples of 3 are 3, 6, 9, 12, … . Multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, … . The first shared multiple is 12.
[WorkedStep] Give 1/3 denominator 12: 1/3 = (1 × 4)/(3 × 4) = 4/12.
[WorkedStep] Give 3/4 denominator 12 too: 3/4 = (3 × 3)/(4 × 3) = 9/12.
[WorkedStep] Add the fraction parts: 4/12 + 9/12 = 13/12.
[WorkedStep] We can take 1 whole out of 13/12: 13/12 = 12/12 + 1/12 = 1 + 1/12.
[WorkedStep] Add the extra whole to the 4 we already had: 4 + 1 + 1/12 = 5 1/12.
[WorkedStep] Our answer, 5 1/12, is close to the estimate of 5.
