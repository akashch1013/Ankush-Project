## Converting Fractions to Decimals
The fraction bar can mean division: 19/100 means 19 ÷ 100. It also tells us we have 19 hundredths.
Ten hundredths make 1 tenth. So 19/100 = 10/100 + 9/100 = 0.1 + 0.09 = 0.19.
The decimal 0.19 stops after the 9. We call a decimal that stops a terminating decimal.
For 1/3, calculate 1 ÷ 3. Share 1 whole among 3 people. Nobody gets a whole, so start with 0. Change 1 whole to 10 tenths: 10 ÷ 3 = 3 remainder 1. Each person gets 0.3, with 1 tenth left.
Change the remaining 1 tenth to 10 hundredths. Again, 10 ÷ 3 = 3 remainder 1. Each person gets another 0.03, with 1 hundredth left.
We keep getting remainder 1. Each exchange gives 10 of the next smaller place, and 10 ÷ 3 again gives 3 remainder 1. So the digit 3 keeps coming: 1/3 = 0.333… . The dots say, “keep going”.
A decimal with a digit or block of digits that repeats forever is called recurring. Here, 0.333… repeats the digit 3. Going on forever is not enough: there must be a repeating pattern.

Example 11
[Question] Express these fractions as decimals.
[Question] (a)  3/4
[Question] (b)  3/2
[Question] (c)  2 1/10
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) To change 3/4 to a decimal, work out 3 ÷ 4. We will write 3 as 3.00 so there are places ready for the division. Since 3 < 4, the answer begins with 0.
[WorkedStep] Use the tenths next: 3 wholes = 30 tenths. Now 30 ÷ 4 gives 7 tenths, because 4 × 7 = 28. There are 30 − 28 = 2 tenths left. The answer so far is 0.7.
[WorkedStep] Exchange the remaining 2 tenths for 20 hundredths. Then 20 ÷ 4 = 5 hundredths, with nothing left.
[WorkedStep] So each share is 0.7 + 0.05 = 0.75. The written division below records those same steps; the decimal points line up.
