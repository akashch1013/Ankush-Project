## Multiplying a Fraction by a Fraction
In Stage 1, the full square represents 1 whole sheet of paper.
In Stage 2, divide the sheet into 3 equal strips. Shade 1 strip blue: that is 1/3 of the sheet.
In Stage 3, divide the blue strip into 5 equal pieces and choose 1 of them. The dark, hatched piece is 1/5 of the blue 1/3. Extend the same cuts through the rest of the square so we can count the equal pieces in the whole sheet.

[Caption] Take 1/5 of the shaded 1/3: 1 of the 15 equal cells.

Select first-column cells in the 1 blue rows using dark teal and hatching. Exactly 1 selected cells; product 1/15. No other value or later example shown. Mathematical accuracy and comprehension take priority.
Count the whole sheet: 3 rows × 5 columns = 15 equal pieces. We chose 1 dark piece, so the answer is 1/15 of the sheet.
We write “of” as multiplication here: 1/3 × 1/5 = (1 × 1)/(3 × 5) = 1/15.
The diagram explains the calculation. Multiplying the denominators, 3 × 5, gives the 15 equal parts in the whole. Multiplying the numerators, 1 × 1, gives the 1 part chosen.
So, to multiply fractions, multiply top by top and bottom by bottom, then simplify. The next example shows this with 2/3 × 1/5.
Notice that 1/15 is smaller than 1/3. Taking only 1/5 of a positive amount makes it smaller. Multiplication does not always make a number bigger!
