# 2.6 Rounding
Sometimes a question asks us to use fewer decimal digits. We choose a nearby number with the number of decimal places requested. This is called rounding.
Decimal places are the places after the decimal point. For example, 6.348 has 3 decimal places: 3 is in the tenths place, 4 in the hundredths place, and 8 in the thousandths place. The short form d.p. means decimal places.
We use ≈ to mean “is approximately equal to”. It tells us we have used a rounded value, rather than the exact original number. Let’s see how to choose that value on a number line.
Example 17
[Question] Round as requested.
[Question] (a)  6.348 to the nearest whole number
[Question] (b)  0.39 to 1 decimal place
[Question] (c)  4.7897 to 3 decimal places
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) We need a whole number near 6.348. The whole numbers on either side are 6 and 7. Halfway between them is 6.5.
[WorkedStep] Look at the blue point marked 6.348 on the number line. It is to the left of 6.5, so it is closer to 6 than to 7.
[WorkedStep] Answer: 6.348 ≈ 6 to the nearest whole number.
