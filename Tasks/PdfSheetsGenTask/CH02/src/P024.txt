Practice 2A Question 9
[Question] Arrange 3/2, 4/3, 5/4 and 6/5 in ascending order. What do you observe?
Check your method
[WorkedStep] Write each fraction as a mixed number: 3/2 = 1 1/2, 4/3 = 1 1/3, 5/4 = 1 1/4, and 6/5 = 1 1/5.
[WorkedStep] Each whole-number part is 1, so compare 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 and 1/5. The next picture uses equal-sized rectangles for 1 whole. Each row shades 1 part.
[ProductionLabel] DO NOT PRINT — Figure reference: V05a-R7 | The following caption sentence is printable unchanged.
[Caption] More equal parts in the same whole make each part smaller.
[ProductionNote] Production note V05a-R7 — Four identical-width bars partitioned into 2,3,4,5 equal pieces; shade exactly one in each. Explicitly identify each reference bar as the same whole. Label 1/2,1/3,1/4,1/5 and the ascending comparison. Do not vary whole widths. Use the current ChandRush colour and design style wherever it supports understanding. If that style would reduce clarity, retain the teaching colours, contrast and mathematical design required here. Mathematical accuracy and comprehension take priority.
[WorkedStep] One of 5 equal parts is smaller than one of 4 equal parts, and so on: 1/5 < 1/4 < 1/3 < 1/2.
[WorkedStep] Add the whole-number part 1 to each: 1 1/5 < 1 1/4 < 1 1/3 < 1 1/2.
[WorkedStep] Ascending order in the original fractions: 6/5, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2.
[WorkedStep] Notice the pattern: 3/2, 4/3, 5/4, 6/5 each have 1 more on top than underneath. Each is 1 plus a smaller fraction: 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, then 1/5. That is why these four numbers get smaller. Adding 1 to both parts of a fraction does not always make it smaller.
