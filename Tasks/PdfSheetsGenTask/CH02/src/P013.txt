[PanelizationLabel] ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT
Now Grandpa has a box of 12 pies. He wants to give away 3/4 of those 12 pies. This time, “the whole” means all 12 pies together. We will divide the group, not cut each pie.
Look at the picture: each small circle represents 1 pie. The 4 boxes put the 12 pies into 4 equal groups. Each group contains 3 pies.

[Caption] The whole group contains 12 pies. Each of its four equal groups contains 3 pies.
[ProductionNote] Production note V02 — Show 12 equal labelled pie symbols arranged as four visibly separated groups of three. Mark three groups teal, one unfilled; do not depict 3/4 of a single pie. Use the current ChandRush colour and design style wherever it supports understanding. If that style would reduce clarity, retain the teaching colours, contrast and mathematical design required here. Mathematical accuracy and comprehension take priority.
Find 1/4 of the 12 pies: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 pies.
We need 3/4, so take 3 of those groups: 3 × 3 = 9 pies.
We can record both steps as 3/4 × 12 = 9 pies.
Grandpa gives away 9 pies. Check: 3 pies stay in the box, and 9 + 3 = 12 pies.
Notice the difference: 1/4 of 1 pie is a piece of pie, but 1/4 of 12 pies is 3 pies. Before using a fraction, say what you are taking a fraction of.
Next we will count amounts such as 1 whole pie and half of another pie. Later we will write amounts as decimals and percentages too. Keep a pencil nearby. At each “Your turn”, try the question before reading its worked answer.
