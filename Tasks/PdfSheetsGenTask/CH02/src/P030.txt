[PanelizationLabel] ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT
Now let’s add 3 1/6 and 2 1/6. We have two jobs: add the whole-number parts, then add the fraction parts.
Example 5
[Question] Evaluate each sum.
[Question] (a)  3 1/6 + 2 1/6
[Question] (b)  2 1/8 + 1 7/8
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) Start with 3 1/6 + 2 1/6. Keep the whole numbers and fractions separate for a moment.
[WorkedStep] First add the whole-number parts: 3 + 2 = 5.
[WorkedStep] Next add the fraction parts: 1/6 + 1/6 = (1 + 1)/6 = 2/6.
[WorkedStep] Put those results together: 3 1/6 + 2 1/6 = 5 2/6.
[WorkedStep] Now simplify the fraction part, 2/6. Divide its top and bottom by 2: 2/6 = (2 ÷ 2)/(6 ÷ 2) = 1/3.
[WorkedStep] So our answer is 5 2/6 = 5 1/3.
[WorkedStep] Our total, 5 1/3, is a little more than 3 + 2 = 5, as we expected.
[WorkedStep] (b) Now add 2 1/8 + 1 7/8. Add the whole-number parts: 2 + 1 = 3. Add the fraction parts: 1/8 + 7/8 = 8/8 = 1.
[WorkedStep] The fraction parts have made 1 extra whole: 8/8 = 1. Add that to the 3 wholes.
[WorkedStep] 2 1/8 + 1 7/8 = 3 + 8/8 = 3 + 1 = 4.
[WorkedStep] Check with a nearby estimate: 2 1/8 is near 2 and 1 7/8 is near 2, so a total of 4 makes sense.
Your turn after Example 5
[Question] Evaluate and simplify.
[Question] (a)  4 3/7 + 2 2/7
[Question] (b)  4 3/5 + 1 4/5
Check your method
[WorkedStep] (a) 4 3/7 + 2 2/7 = (4 + 2) + (3 + 2)/7 = 6 5/7.
[WorkedStep] (b) 4 3/5 + 1 4/5 = 5 + 7/5.
[WorkedStep] Here 7/5 = 5/5 + 2/5 = 1 2/5, so the total is 5 + 1 2/5 = 6 2/5.
