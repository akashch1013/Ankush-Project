[PanelizationLabel] ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT
The number before % can itself contain a fraction or a decimal. For example, 5 1/2% means 5 1/2 ÷ 100, and 50.2% means 50.2 ÷ 100. Keep the entire number, including its fraction or decimal part, when dividing by 100.
Example 30
[Question] Express each percentage as a fraction in simplest form.
[Question] (a)  5 1/2%
[Question] (b)  50.2%
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) First write the mixed number as an improper fraction: 5 1/2 = (5 × 2 + 1)/2 = 11/2.
[WorkedStep] Now apply the % sign: divide 11/2 by 100. The reciprocal of 100 is 1/100, so dividing by 100 is multiplying by 1/100:
[WorkedStep] 5 1/2% = 11/2 ÷ 100 = 11/2 × 1/100 = (11 × 1)/(2 × 100) = 11/200.
[WorkedStep] Check: 5% = 5/100 = 10/200. Our answer, 11/200, is a little greater than 10/200, just as 5 1/2% is a little greater than 5%.
[WorkedStep] (b) 50.2% means 50.2/100. Multiply top and bottom by 10 so both are whole numbers: 50.2/100 = (50.2 × 10)/(100 × 10) = 502/1000.
[WorkedStep] Now divide both by 2: 502/1000 = (502 ÷ 2)/(1000 ÷ 2) = 251/500. Since 50.2% is slightly more than 50%, this fraction should be slightly more than 1/2. It is: half of 500 is 250, and our top number is 251.
Your turn after Example 30
[Question] Express each percentage as a fraction in simplest form.
[Question] (a)  11 1/4%
[Question] (b)  10.5%
Check your method
[WorkedStep] (a)  11 1/4 = (11 × 4 + 1)/4 = 45/4. So 11 1/4% = 45/4 × 1/100 = 45/400 = (45 ÷ 5)/(400 ÷ 5) = 9/80.
[WorkedStep] (b)  10.5% = 10.5/100 = (10.5 × 10)/(100 × 10) = 105/1000 = (105 ÷ 5)/(1000 ÷ 5) = 21/200. The same “divide by 100” meaning works for both parts.
