Check your method
[WorkedStep] (a) In 10.215, keep 10.21 for 2 decimal places. The next digit is 5, so add 0.01: 10.21 + 0.01 = 10.22. Thus 10.215 ≈ 10.22.
[WorkedStep] (b) In 0.948, keep the whole-number part 0. The next digit, the tenths digit, is 9. Round up from 0 to 1: 0.948 ≈ 1.
[WorkedStep] (c) In 4.95, keep 4.9 for 1 decimal place. The next digit is 5, so add 0.1: 4.9 + 0.1 = 5.0. Thus 4.95 ≈ 5.0. Write 5.0 to show 1 decimal place.
[WorkedStep] If we start with a fraction, first divide its numerator by its denominator. Work out at least one more decimal digit than the question asks us to keep. That extra digit tells us whether to round up.

[Question] Express each fraction as a decimal rounded as requested.
[Question] (a)  1/6 to 1 decimal place
[Question] (b)  22/7 to 2 decimal places
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) For 1/6 to 1 decimal place, we need the first two decimal digits. Use 1 ÷ 6 = 0.1666… .
[WorkedStep] Keep 0.1 and look at the next digit, 6. It tells us to round up: 0.1 + 0.1 = 0.2. So 1/6 ≈ 0.2 to 1 d.p.
[WorkedStep] (b) For 22/7 to 2 decimal places, use 22 ÷ 7 = 3.142857… . Keep 3.14; the next digit is 2, so leave 3.14 unchanged.
[WorkedStep] Thus 22/7 ≈ 3.14 to 2 d.p. Read one extra digit before rounding; the rest can wait.
