[PanelizationLabel] ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT
[WorkedStep] Splitting a number can help with multiplication too. For example, 3 × 11 = 3 × (10 + 1) = 3 × 10 + 3 × 1 = 30 + 3 = 33. We multiply both parts, 10 and 1, by 3. This is called the distributive law.
[WorkedStep] We may also swap the numbers in a multiplication: 2 × 5 = 5 × 2 = 10. This is called the commutative law. We will use it to put easy multiplications first.
[WorkedStep] Swapping does not work for subtraction or division: 8 − 2 = 6, but 2 − 8 = −6; 8 ÷ 2 = 4, but 2 ÷ 8 = 1/4.
Example 24
[Question] Calculate without a calculator.
[Question] (a)  6.8 × 11
[Question] (b)  0.5 × 19 × 10
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) For 6.8 × 11, write 11 as 10 + 1. Then multiply 6.8 by both parts:
[WorkedStep] 6.8 × 11 = 6.8 × 10 + 6.8 × 1 = 68 + 6.8 = 74.8.
[WorkedStep] (b) In 0.5 × 19 × 10, all the signs are multiplication signs, so we can change the order. Multiply 0.5 by 10 first:
[WorkedStep] 0.5 × 19 × 10 = 0.5 × 10 × 19 = 5 × 19.
[WorkedStep] Now use 19 = 20 − 1. Twenty lots of 5 make 100; remove 1 lot of 5 to leave 19 lots:
[WorkedStep] 5 × 19 = 5 × 20 − 5 × 1 = 100 − 5 = 95.
Your turn after Example 24

[Question] (a)  8.4 × 9
[Question] (b)  1.3 × 21 × 100
Check your method
[WorkedStep] (a)  8.4 × 9 = 8.4 × (10 − 1) = 84 − 8.4 = 75.6.
[WorkedStep] (b)  Reorder: 1.3 × 100 × 21 = 130 × 21. Split 21: 130 × 20 + 130 × 1 = 2600 + 130 = 2730.
