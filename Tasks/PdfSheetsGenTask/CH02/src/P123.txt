# 2.8 Expressing a Percentage as a Fraction or Decimal
Fractions and decimals have given us two ways to describe the same amount. A sale sign introduces another. Suppose an ice cream usually costs $4 and the sign says “50% off”. Does that mean it now costs 50 cents? The small % sign makes a big difference. It means “out of 100”, not “cents”.
50% means 50/100 = 1/2. So “50% off” removes half of the original $4 price. The reduction is $4 ÷ 2 = $2. Subtract it: $4 − $2 = $2. The new price is $2; the sign does not mean “50 cents off”.
## Knowledge Building Task
Look for percentage discounts in a newspaper, a magazine, a poster or a shop. Record the item and its percentage discount. Compare the percentages and find the greatest and least in your collection. You can do this alone or with a partner; use real offers you can actually see.
Here is a set to practise with: shampoo 15% off, soap 10% less, biscuits 25% discount and cola 20% off. Which has the greatest percentage discount? Which has the least? Pause and compare the numbers.
Put the discount numbers in order: 10 < 15 < 20 < 25. The biscuits have the greatest percentage discount, 25%, and the soap has the least, 10%. To find which offer saves the most money, we would also need each original price. For example, 10% of $100 saves $10, but 25% of $4 saves only $1.
