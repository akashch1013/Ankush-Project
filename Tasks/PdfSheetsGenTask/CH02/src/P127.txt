A percentage can also be written as a decimal. The % sign still tells us to divide by 100. Let’s watch the place values.
Example 31
[Question] Express each percentage as a decimal.
[Question] (a)  5%
[Question] (b)  115%
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a)  5% means 5/100. That is five hundredths, so 5% = 0.05. Put a zero in the tenths place; 0.5 would mean fifty hundredths, or 50%.
[WorkedStep] (b)  115% = 115/100 = 1 + 15/100 = 1.15. Check: 100% supplies the 1 whole and the extra 15% supplies 0.15.
Your turn after Example 31
[Question] Express each percentage as a decimal.
[Question] (a)  90%
[Question] (b)  180%
Check your method
[WorkedStep] (a)  90% = 90 ÷ 100 = 0.9.
[WorkedStep] (b)  180% = 180 ÷ 100 = 1.8.

Example 32
[Question] Express each percentage as a decimal.
[Question] (a)  15 1/2%
[Question] (b)  38.3%
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] (a) Since 1/2 = 0.5, the number 15 1/2 becomes 15 + 0.5 = 15.5.
[WorkedStep] Now divide 15.5 by 100. We can divide by 10 twice: 15.5 ÷ 10 = 1.55, then 1.55 ÷ 10 = 0.155. Therefore 15 1/2% = 15.5 ÷ 100 = 0.155.
[WorkedStep] (b) 38.3% = 38.3 ÷ 100 = 0.383.
[WorkedStep] Check: 15 1/2% and 38.3% are both below 100%, and their decimals, 0.155 and 0.383, are both below 1.
Your turn after Example 32
[Question] Express each percentage as a decimal.
[Question] (a)  5 3/4%
[Question] (b)  72.5%
Check your method
[WorkedStep] (a)  3/4 = (3 × 25)/(4 × 25) = 75/100 = 0.75, so 5 3/4% = 5.75 ÷ 100 = 0.0575.
[WorkedStep] (b) 72.5% = 72.5 ÷ 100 = 0.725.
We can now write a percentage as a fraction or a decimal. Next, let’s look carefully at what the percentage describes: books in a library, or extra bread in a packet.

Example 33
[Question] In a library, 40% of the books are storybooks. Express the number of storybooks as a fraction of all the books.
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] Here the whole collection means all the books. 40% = 40/100 = (40 ÷ 20)/(100 ÷ 20) = 2/5.
[WorkedStep] So 2/5 of the books are storybooks. This describes a fraction of the collection, not a claim that the library has only 100 books.
Your turn after Example 33
[Question] In the same library, 55% of the books are non-English books. Express this as a fraction of all the books.
Check your method
[WorkedStep] 55% = 55/100 = (55 ÷ 5)/(100 ÷ 5) = 11/20. So 11/20 of all the books are non-English books.
