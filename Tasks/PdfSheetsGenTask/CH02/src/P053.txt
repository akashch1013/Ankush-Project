Practice 2C Question 4
[Question] Sarah claims that 2/5 × 3/8 = 6/4. Without calculating the product, explain whether she is correct.
Check your method
[WorkedStep] Sarah is not correct. In 2/5 × 3/8, we take only 3/8 of 2/5. Since 3/8 is less than 1, the answer must be smaller than 2/5.
[WorkedStep] But her answer, 6/4, is greater than 1 because 6 > 4. The starting amount, 2/5, is less than 1 because 2 < 5. Her answer is too large. We have checked this without calculating the product.
Practice 2C Question 5
[Question] Ali claims that 6/7 ÷ 3/5 = 2/15. Without calculating, explain whether he is correct. If he is wrong, correct his answer.
Check your method
[WorkedStep] Ali is not correct. Dividing 6/7 by 3/5, a positive fraction below 1, must give an answer greater than 6/7.
[WorkedStep] His answer, 2/15, is less than 1/2, while 6/7 is greater than 1/2. So 2/15 is smaller than 6/7, the opposite of what we need. We can reject his answer before calculating the division.
[WorkedStep] Now correct it: 6/7 ÷ 3/5 = 6/7 × 5/3 = ((6 ÷ 3) × 5)/(7 × (3 ÷ 3)) = 10/7 = 1 3/7.
[WorkedStep] Check: 10/7 × 3/5 = 30/35 = 6/7.

Practice 2C Question 6
[Question] A lorry has 3/7 tonne of rice. It is packed into bags holding 3/112 tonne each. How many complete bags can be filled? Explain why the answer is a whole number.
Check your method
[WorkedStep] We have 3/7 tonne of rice, and each bag needs 3/112 tonne. Give the total denominator 112: 3/7 = (3 × 16)/(7 × 16) = 48/112 tonne.
[WorkedStep] Think of the total as 48 equal portions, each weighing 1/112 tonne. Each bag needs 3 such portions.
[WorkedStep] 48 ÷ 3 = 16, so there are 16 complete bags. Equivalently, 3/7 ÷ 3/112 = 3/7 × 112/3 = 112/7 = 16.
[WorkedStep] The answer is a whole number because the total contains exactly 16 bag-sized groups, with no rice left. Check: 16 × 3/112 = 48/112 = 3/7 tonne.
