Example 15
[Question] Arrange 0.314, 310/1000 and 3 14/100 in ascending order.
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] First write the fractions as decimals: 310/1000 = 0.310, and 3 14/100 = 3 + 0.14 = 3.14.
[WorkedStep] Compare 0.314, 0.310 and 3.14. The number 3.14 is greatest because it has 3 wholes, while the other two have 0 wholes.
[WorkedStep] Now compare 0.310 and 0.314. Both have 3 tenths and 1 hundredth. Their thousandths digits differ: 0 < 4, so 0.310 < 0.314.
[WorkedStep] Thus 0.310 < 0.314 < 3.14. In the original forms, the ascending order is 310/1000, 0.314, 3 14/100.
Your turn after Example 15
[Question] Arrange 0.123, 1 23/100 and 132/1000 in ascending order.
Check your method
[WorkedStep] 1 23/100 = 1.23 and 132/1000 = 0.132. Compare 0.123 < 0.132 < 1.23.
[WorkedStep] Ascending order: 0.123, 132/1000, 1 23/100.

Example 16
[Question] Arrange 5.25, 525/1000 and 5 3/4 in descending order.
Let’s work it through
[WorkedStep] Write 525/1000 = 0.525. For 5 3/4, change the fraction part: 3/4 = (3 × 25)/(4 × 25) = 75/100 = 0.75, so 5 3/4 = 5.75.
[WorkedStep] Now compare 5.25, 0.525 and 5.75. Both 5.25 and 5.75 are greater than 0.525 because their whole-number part is 5, not 0. Between 5.25 and 5.75, compare tenths: 7 > 2, so 5.75 is greater.
[WorkedStep] Descending order: 5.75 > 5.25 > 0.525. In the original forms: 5 3/4, 5.25, 525/1000.
Your turn after Example 16
[Question] Arrange 20.3, 203/1000 and 20 33/100 in descending order.
Check your method
[WorkedStep] 20.3 = 20.30; 203/1000 = 0.203; 20 33/100 = 20.33.
[WorkedStep] Descending order: 20 33/100, 20.3, 203/1000.
