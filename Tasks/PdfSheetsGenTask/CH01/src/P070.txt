[MathExample] 6 Check Susan’s claim
Susan claims that √32 + √25 = √(32 + 25) = √57. Is she correct? Explain.
[AnswerLead] Check your thinking
No. In √32 + √25, we need two separate roots and then their sum. Let’s compare that with Susan’s √57:
Use a calculator to compare the two results. √32 is about 5.66; the symbol ≈ means “is approximately equal to”. So:
[Equation] √32 + √25 ≈ 5.66 + 5 = 10.66
[Equation] √(32 + 25) = √57 ≈ 7.55
About 10.66 is quite different from about 7.55. Susan’s first equals sign is the mistake: adding two roots is not the same as adding first and taking one root.

[AnswerLead] Check your method
The bar of √ covers the whole sum 5² + 12². Work out that sum before taking its root:
[Equation] c = √(5² + 12²) = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13
So c = 13. Check: 13² = 169 and 5² + 12² = 169. The sloping side is longer than both 5 and 12, as the triangle suggests.
