[MathExample] 6 Find the smallest shared multiple
Write down the smallest number divisible by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 25 and 100.
[AnswerLead] Check your method
Here “smallest” means the smallest positive number, as in our LCM work. This looks like eleven separate jobs, but some are already covered by others.
Start with 100: 100 ÷ 2 = 50, 100 ÷ 4 = 25, 100 ÷ 5 = 20, 100 ÷ 10 = 10 and 100 ÷ 25 = 4. So every multiple of 100 already fits those five divisors.
A multiple of 9 also divides by 3, because 9 = 3 × 3. If it is also even, it passes the 6-test. That leaves us looking for the first shared multiple of 100, 8, 9 and 7.
First fit 100 and 8. Try 100, then 200:
[Equation] 100 = 8 × 12 + 4
[Equation] 200 = 8 × 25
100 leaves 4 over; 200 divides exactly. So the LCM of 100 and 8 is 200. Their shared multiples are 200, 400, 600, ….
