[MathExample] 8 Compare the roots
Fill each blank with < or >. What do you notice?
(a) √64 □ ∛64 (b) √729 □ ∛729
(c) √70 □ ∛70 (d) ∛65 □ √65
[AnswerLead] Check your method
(a) √64 = 8 and ∛64 = 4. Since 8 > 4, √64 > ∛64.
(b) √729 = 27 because 27² = 729; ∛729 = 9 because 9³ = 729. Since 27 > 9, √729 > ∛729.
(c) 70 is not a perfect square or cube. Let’s trap each root between numbers we know.
For √70, 8 × 8 = 64 is too small and 9 × 9 = 81 is too large. So √70 lies between 8 and 9.
For ∛70, 4³ = 64 is too small and 5³ = 125 is too large. So ∛70 lies between 4 and 5.
Anything between 8 and 9 is greater than anything between 4 and 5. Therefore √70 > ∛70.
(d) Use the same nearby powers for 65: 4³ = 64 < 65 < 125 = 5³, while 8² = 64 < 65 < 81 = 9². So ∛65 is between 4 and 5, and √65 is between 8 and 9. The cube root comes first here, so ∛65 < √65.
In all four examples, the square root is greater than the cube root. This comparison holds for numbers greater than 1. It is not a rule for every number: at 1 the two roots are equal, for example.
