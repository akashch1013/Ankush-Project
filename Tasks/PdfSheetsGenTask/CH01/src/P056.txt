[MathSubsection] Practice 1C
Keep the tests beside you. When several tests use the digit sum, calculate that sum once and reuse it.
[MathExample] 1 Use divisibility tests
Check whether (a) 12349 is divisible by 9, (b) 14565 is divisible by 5 and (c) 448 is divisible by 7.
[AnswerLead] Check your method
(a) 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 9 = 19. Since 19 is not divisible by 9, no.
(b) 14565 ends in 5, so yes.
(c) 44 − (8 × 2) = 44 − 16 = 28. Since 28 = 7 × 4, yes.

[MathExample] 2 Check all eleven divisors
For each number, check divisibility by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 25 and 100.
(a) 28350  (b) 15876  (c) 7654320
[AnswerLead] Check your method
First gather the few facts you will reuse:
(a) 28350 has digit sum 2 + 8 + 3 + 5 + 0 = 18, last two digits 50 and last three digits 350. For 7:
[Equation] 2835 − 2 × 0 = 2835
[Equation] 283 − 2 × 5 = 273
[Equation] 27 − 2 × 3 = 21
Since 21 is divisible by 7, 28350 passes that test.
(b) 15876 has digit sum 1 + 5 + 8 + 7 + 6 = 27, ending 76 and final three digits 876. For 7:
[Equation] 1587 − 2 × 6 = 1575
[Equation] 157 − 2 × 5 = 147
[Equation] 14 − 2 × 7 = 0
0 is divisible by 7, so 15876 passes.
(c) 7654320 has digit sum 7 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 0 = 27, ending 20 and final three digits 320. For 7:
[Equation] 765432 − 2 × 0 = 765432
[Equation] 76543 − 2 × 2 = 76539
[Equation] 7653 − 2 × 9 = 7635
[Equation] 763 − 2 × 5 = 753
[Equation] 75 − 2 × 3 = 69
69 is not divisible by 7, so 7654320 fails that test.
