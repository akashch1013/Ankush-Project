[MathExample] Think about the product
Why is the product of two positive integers always a common multiple of those integers?
[AnswerLead] Check your thinking
Take 4 and 6. Their product is 4 × 6 = 24. Since 24 ÷ 4 = 6 and 24 ÷ 6 = 4, it is a multiple of both. The same reasoning works for any two positive integers: divide their product by one factor and you get the other factor, with no remainder.
That product need not be the least common multiple. For 4 and 6, 12 is already a common multiple, and 12 is smaller than 24.

[MathExample] Now you try
Find the LCM of 4, 6 and 9.
[AnswerLead] Check your method
[Equation] Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36
[Equation] Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36
[Equation] Multiples of 9: 9, 18, 27, 36
The first number in all three lists is 36, so LCM(4, 6, 9) = 36.
