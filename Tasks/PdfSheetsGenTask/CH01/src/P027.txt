[MathExample] 6 Apples
Sarah has 7 apples. She gives 4 away and buys another 3. How many apples does she have?
[AnswerLead] Check your method
Sarah starts with 7 apples. She gives away 4, leaving 3, then buys 3 more:
[Equation] 7 − 4 + 3 = 3 + 3 = 6
Sarah has 6 apples.

[MathExample] 7 Paying for a meal
Sean has $10. He must pay $8 for a meal and $3 for a drink. How much more money does he need?
[AnswerLead] Check your method
The total cost is $8 + $3 = $11. Compare that with his $10:
[Equation] 11 − 10 = 1
Sean needs $1 more. Alternatively, 10 − 8 − 3 = −1 shows a shortage of $1; the amount he needs is positive $1.

[MathExample] 8 Tell a story with numbers
Tell a story about buying and selling oranges for each expression.
(a) 16 + (−3) + 5  (b) 14 + 2 − 10
[AnswerLead] Check your method
(a) One possible answer: “A seller has 16 oranges, sells 3, then buys 5 more. How many oranges remain?” Selling 3 is a change of −3:
[Equation] 16 + (−3) + 5 = 16 − 3 + 5 = 13 + 5 = 18
(b) One possible answer: “A seller has 14 oranges, buys 2 more, then sells 10. How many oranges remain?”
[Equation] 14 + 2 − 10 = 16 − 10 = 6
Your story may be different. Check that the starting number, changes and order match the expression, and that every number counts oranges.
