[MathSubsection] Order of operations
When several operations share an expression, use this order:
1. Work inside brackets first, starting with the innermost brackets. Follow these same rules inside them.
2. Find powers and roots.
3. Multiply and divide from left to right.
4. Add and subtract from left to right.
Multiplication and division share a place in the queue. Do whichever comes first from the left:
[Equation] 12 ÷ 3 × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8
Division comes first in that example. Addition and subtraction also share a place:
[Equation] 10 − 7 + 2 = 3 + 2 = 5

[MathSubsection] Squares and square roots
Squaring multiplies a number by itself. A square root reverses that operation; the √ sign gives the root that is positive or zero:
[Equation] 4² = 4 × 4 = 16
[Equation] √144 = √(12 × 12) = 12
[MathSubsection] Cubes and cube roots
Cubing uses three equal factors. A cube root finds the repeated factor:
[Equation] 8³ = 8 × 8 × 8 = 512
[Equation] ∛729 = ∛(9 × 9 × 9) = 9
