Cutting the largest squares

A rectangular piece of cardboard is 64 cm long and 28 cm wide. Cut it into equal squares with no cardboard left over. What is the greatest possible side length of each square? How many squares will there be? Explain your answer.

Check your method

The side of a square must fit exactly along both 64 cm and 28 cm. We need the greatest common factor:

Factors of 28: 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28. Of these, 1, 2 and 4 also divide 64. So HCF(64, 28) = 4: each square has side 4 cm.

Now count squares in each direction:

Along the length:64 ÷ 4 = 16
Along the width:28 ÷ 4 = 7
Total:16 × 7 = 112 squares
