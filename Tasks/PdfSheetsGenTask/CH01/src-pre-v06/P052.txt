By 7: Separate the last digit, double it, and subtract that doubled amount from the number made by the other digits. If the result is divisible by 7, the original number is too. Repeat the process if the new number is still large.
For 126, the last digit is 6 and the remaining number is 12. Double 6 to get 12, then subtract:
[Equation] 12 − (6 × 2) = 12 − 12 = 0
0 is divisible by 7 because 0 = 7 × 0. So 126 passes the 7-test. Here are three more applications:
[Equation] 231: 23 − (1 × 2) = 21 = 7 × 3 → yes
[Equation] 155: 15 − (5 × 2) = 5 → no
[Equation] 244: 24 − (4 × 2) = 16 → no
For 231, keep the 23 together. This test uses the remaining number, not the sum 2 + 3.

By 8: Check the number formed by the last three digits. This is like the 4-test, using thousands: 1000 = 8 × 125. In 7656 = 7000 + 656, the 7000 already divides by 8. We only need to check 656:
[Equation] 7656: 656 ÷ 8 = 82 → yes
[Equation] 14824: 824 ÷ 8 = 103 → yes
[Equation] 1273: 273 ÷ 8 leaves a remainder of 1 → no
[Equation] 4825: 825 ÷ 8 leaves a remainder of 1 → no
