[MathSubsection] Taking away a negative amount
Suppose your displayed score is 4. A five-point penalty has already been taken from your score. Now that penalty is cancelled. The five points return, so your score becomes 9.
[Equation] 4 − (−5) = 4 + 5 = 9
Removing the −5 penalty gives the 5 points back. That is why subtracting a negative number has the same effect as adding its positive partner.
[SmallCaption] An applied penalty is removed
[Production] Production note V04. Show a before state with displayed score 4 and a record labelled −5 penalty already applied. Show an after state with that record cancelled and displayed score 9. Join the states with restore 5 points. Do not imply that cancelling an unapplied penalty adds points. Use the current ChandRush colour and design style wherever it supports understanding. If that style would reduce clarity, retain the teaching colours, contrast and mathematical design required here. Mathematical accuracy and comprehension take priority.
On a number line, subtracting −5 undoes a move of five intervals left. Undoing that move takes us five intervals right.

[MathExample] Example 4
Evaluate (a) 0 − (−4), (b) 5 − (−2) and (c) −3 − (−5).
[AnswerLead] Let’s work it through
(a) Subtracting −4 restores 4:
[Equation] 0 − (−4) = 0 + 4 = 4
(b) Subtracting −2 restores 2:
[Equation] 5 − (−2) = 5 + 2 = 7
(c) Start at −3 and move five intervals right. Three steps reach 0; two more reach 2.
[Equation] −3 − (−5) = −3 + 5 = 2
We can check (c) by adding the removed number back: 2 + (−5) = −3. That rebuilds the starting value.

[MathExample] Now you try
Evaluate (a) 0 − (−5), (b) 7 − (−8) and (c) −2 − (−1).
[AnswerLead] Check your method
[Equation] (a) 0 − (−5) = 0 + 5 = 5
[Equation] (b) 7 − (−8) = 7 + 8 = 15
[Equation] (c) −2 − (−1) = −2 + 1 = −1
The answer in (c) is still negative. Subtracting a negative makes the value greater; it does not promise a positive answer.
