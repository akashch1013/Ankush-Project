[MathExample] Example 9
Find the HCF of 12 and 15.
[AnswerLead] Let’s work it through
[Equation] 12 = 1 × 12 = 2 × 6 = 3 × 4
[Equation] 15 = 1 × 15 = 3 × 5
Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15.
The common factors are 1 and 3. Choose the greater one: HCF(12, 15) = 3. Both 12 ÷ 3 = 4 and 15 ÷ 3 = 5 leave no remainder.

[MathExample] Now you try
Find the HCF of 36 and 117.
[AnswerLead] Check your method
The factors of 36 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. For 117:
[Equation] 117 = 1 × 117 = 3 × 39 = 9 × 13
So the factors of 117 are 1, 3, 9, 13, 39, 117. Both 36 and 117 have factors 1, 3 and 9. The greatest is 9, so HCF(36, 117) = 9.

[MathExample] A factor that always works
One positive number is a common factor of every pair of positive integers. Which number is it, and why?
[AnswerLead] Check your thinking
It is 1. Every positive integer divides by 1 exactly: for example, 36 ÷ 1 = 36 and 117 ÷ 1 = 117. That is why a pair can have HCF 1, but cannot have “no common factor”.
