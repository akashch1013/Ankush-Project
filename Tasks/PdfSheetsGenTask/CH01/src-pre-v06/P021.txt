[MathExample] Example 6
Evaluate (a) −3 × 2 and (b) 2 × (−3).
[AnswerLead] Let’s work it through
In both calculations the signs are different. Multiply 3 × 2 = 6 and give the product a minus sign:
[Equation] (a) −3 × 2 = −(3 × 2) = −6
[Equation] (b) 2 × (−3) = −(2 × 3) = −6

One useful extension. What if both factors are negative? Start with a pattern we can already calculate:
[Equation] (−3) × 2 = −6
[Equation] (−3) × 1 = −3
[Equation] (−3) × 0 = 0
[Equation] (−3) × (−1) = 3
[Equation] (−3) × (−2) = 6
Read the answers down the column: −6, −3, 0, 3, 6. They rise by 3 each time. Keeping that pattern going past zero gives (−3) × (−2) = 6: two negative factors give a positive product.
For two non-zero factors, same signs give a positive product; different signs give a negative product. Multiplying by zero gives zero. Keep this rule with multiplication: −3 + (−2) is −5, even though the two numbers being added have the same sign.
