[MathExample] 6 Cube a sum or add two cubes
(a) Use a calculator to evaluate each pair:
(i) (1 + 2)³ and 1³ + 2³
(ii) (2 + 3)³ and 2³ + 3³
(iii) (3 + 5)³ and 3³ + 5³
(iv) (4 + 6)³ and 4³ + 6³
(b) What do you notice about the results?
(c) Which statements are true?
(i) (3 + 5)³ > 3³ + 5³
(ii) (3 + 5)³ ≠ 3³ + 5³
(iii) (3 + 5)³ < 3³ + 5³

[AnswerLead] Check your method
(a)(i) Brackets make us add 1 + 2 first in the first expression. In the second expression, cube 1 and 2 separately:
[Equation] (1 + 2)³ = 3³ = 27
[Equation] 1³ + 2³ = 1 + 8 = 9
Use the same distinction for the other pairs:
[Equation] (ii) (2 + 3)³ = 5³ = 125;   2³ + 3³ = 8 + 27 = 35
[Equation] (iii) (3 + 5)³ = 8³ = 512;   3³ + 5³ = 27 + 125 = 152
[Equation] (iv) (4 + 6)³ = 10³ = 1000;   4³ + 6³ = 64 + 216 = 280
(b) In every pair here, cubing the sum gives a greater result than adding the separate cubes. These are different calculations, so we must not swap one for the other.
(c) We found 512 and 152. Since 512 > 152 and 512 ≠ 152, statements (i) and (ii) are true. Statement (iii) is false.
