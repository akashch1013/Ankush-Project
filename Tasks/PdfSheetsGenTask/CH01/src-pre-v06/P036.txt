[MathSubsection] Factors
We have been making multiples, such as 4 × 9 = 36. Now ask the question backwards: “Which positive whole numbers divide 36 exactly?” Those numbers are its factors. For example, 36 ÷ 4 = 9, with no remainder, so 4 is a factor of 36.
We can find factors in pairs. For 36, start with 1 and try the next whole numbers in order:
[Equation] 36 = 1 × 36 = 2 × 18 = 3 × 12 = 4 × 9 = 6 × 6
5 does not divide 36 exactly, so it gives no factor pair. At 6 × 6 the two factors meet; continuing would repeat the pairs in reverse. The complete list is 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36.
Now do the same for 24:
[Equation] 24 = 1 × 24 = 2 × 12 = 3 × 8 = 4 × 6
5 does not work, and 6 × 4 repeats 4 × 6. The factors of 24 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24.
Now compare the two factor lists. Both contain 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12. These are their common factors. Choose the greatest, 12: their highest common factor, or HCF, is 12.
