[MathSubsection] More divisibility tests
Some tests look at the last digits; others use their sum. Let’s take them one at a time. You do not need to memorise all eleven in one heroic gulp.
By 4: Check the number formed by the last two digits. Why can we ignore the earlier digits? A complete hundred divides by 4: 100 = 4 × 25. For 37524, split off the complete hundreds:
[Equation] 37524 = 375 × 100 + 24
The hundreds already divide by 4. The remaining 24 = 4 × 6 does too, so 37524 passes. But 12345 ends in 45, which does not divide by 4, so 12345 fails.

By 6: Look at the multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, … . Every second one is even; those are exactly the multiples of 6. So a number must pass both the 2-test and the 3-test.
For 3456, the last digit 6 is even and 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 = 18 is divisible by 3: both tests pass. For 482, the last digit is even, but 4 + 8 + 2 = 14 is not divisible by 3: the 6-test fails. Passing one test is only half the job.
