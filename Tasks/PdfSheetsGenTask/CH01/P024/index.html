Example 7

Evaluate (a) −45 ÷ 9 and (b) 56 ÷ (−7).

Let’s work it through

Divide the positive amounts, then use the signs:

(a)−45 ÷ 9 = −(45 ÷ 9) = −5
(b)56 ÷ (−7) = −(56 ÷ 7) = −8

Check (b): (−8) × (−7) = 56. The quotient −8 fits.

Evaluate (a) 63 ÷ (−9) and (b) −75 ÷ 15.

Check your method
(a)63 ÷ (−9) = −(63 ÷ 9) = −7
(b)−75 ÷ 15 = −(75 ÷ 15) = −5

One useful extension. For (−12) ÷ (−3), ask what multiplies −3 to give −12. Since 4 × (−3) = −12, the quotient is positive 4.

For division of two non-zero numbers, same signs give a positive quotient; different signs give a negative quotient. Also, 0 ÷ 3 = 0 because 0 × 3 = 0. We cannot divide by zero: for instance, no number multiplied by 0 gives 6, so 6 ÷ 0 has no answer.
