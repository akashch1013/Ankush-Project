Adding and subtracting on a number line

For 6 + 9, start at 6. Adding positive 9 means moving nine equal intervals to the right. You reach 15. For 6 − 9, start at 6 again, but subtracting positive 9 means moving nine intervals to the left. You reach −3.

6 + 9 = 156 − 9 = −3

Count the spaces between ticks when you move. Counting the starting tick as the first step would give you an extra step you never took!

Start at 6 in both calculations

Example 1

Evaluate without a calculator. You may use a calculator afterwards to check.

(a) −7 + 3 (b) −7 − 9

Let’s work it through

(a) Start at −7. Adding 3 takes us three intervals right: −7 → −6 → −5 → −4.

−7 + 3 = −4

(b) Start at −7 again. Subtracting 9 takes us nine intervals left, farther below zero. We are already 7 intervals below zero, and go another 9: 7 + 9 = 16 intervals below zero.

−7 − 9 = −16

Check the second answer by undoing the move: −16 + 9 = −7. We are back at the starting number.

Starting below zero

Now you try

Evaluate without a calculator: (a) −2 + 3 and (b) −2 − 3. You may use a calculator afterwards to check.

Check your method

(a) −2 → −1 → 0 → 1, so −2 + 3 = 1.

(b) Moving three intervals left from −2 gives −3, −4, −5, so −2 − 3 = −5.
