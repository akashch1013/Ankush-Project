(c) Without a calculator, work out (−100) ÷ 4, (−42) ÷ 7, (−25) ÷ 5 and (−18) ÷ 3. What do you notice?

Check your method
(−100) ÷ 4 = −25, because (−25) × 4 = −100
(−42) ÷ 7 = −6, because (−6) × 7 = −42
(−25) ÷ 5 = −5, because (−5) × 5 = −25
(−18) ÷ 3 = −6, because (−6) × 3 = −18

A negative number divided by a positive number gives a negative quotient.

(d) Use a calculator for 100 ÷ (−4), 42 ÷ (−7), 25 ÷ (−5) and 18 ÷ (−3). What do you notice now?

Check your method
100 ÷ (−4) = −25, because (−25) × (−4) = 100
42 ÷ (−7) = −6, because (−6) × (−7) = 42
25 ÷ (−5) = −5, because (−5) × (−5) = 25
18 ÷ (−3) = −6, because (−6) × (−3) = 18

A positive number divided by a negative number also gives a negative quotient. For instance, −25 is the missing factor in (−25) × (−4) = 100.

(e) Use these pairs to check your observations from (c) and (d):

Set A: 45 ÷ (−9) and (−45) ÷ 9.
Set B: 81 ÷ (−27) and (−81) ÷ 27.
Check your method
45 ÷ (−9) = −5; (−45) ÷ 9 = −5
81 ÷ (−27) = −3; (−81) ÷ 27 = −3

In Set A, 45 ÷ 9 = 5; in Set B, 81 ÷ 27 = 3. The signs differ in all four divisions, so the answers are −5 or −3. Both pairs agree with our observations.
