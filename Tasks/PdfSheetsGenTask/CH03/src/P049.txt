TEACHING COPY BEGINS — Production notes remain non-printing Chapter 3 Key Ideas Before the revision, let’s collect the methods with numbers beside them. Name the quantities in order Apples : oranges = 2 : 3 means 2 apple parts for every 3 orange parts. It does not tell us the total number of fruits. Reversing the order gives oranges : apples = 3 : 2. Simplify both terms together 6 : 3 = (6 ÷ 3) : (3 ÷ 3) = 2 : 1 Use whole-number terms with no common factor greater than 1. Multiplying or dividing both terms by the same positive number gives an equivalent ratio: 2 : 3 = (2 × 2) : (3 × 2) = 4 : 6 4 : 6 = (4 × 2) : (6 × 2) = 8 : 12 100 : 150 = (100 ÷ 10) : (150 ÷ 10) = 10 : 15 10 : 15 = (10 ÷ 5) : (15 ÷ 5) = 2 : 3 END PANEL CH03-PAGE049-PANEL01 |

Then 1 part is $30 ÷ 2 = $15, and 3 parts are 3 × $15 = $45. The $30 belongs to the first share; it is not the total. If instead a total is to be shared in the ratio 2 : 3, divide that total into 2 + 3 = 5 equal parts. Multiply the value of one part by 2 and by 3 to find the shares. For direct proportion, the amount per item stays the same. Find one item’s amount, then multiply by the required number of items. END PANEL CH03-PAGE049-PANEL02 |
