END NON-PRINTING CARRIED PRODUCTION CONTEXT ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT Example 4 A carton contains 1/2 L of orange juice. Another contains 5/8 L of apple juice. Find the ratio of the volume of orange juice to the volume of apple juice. Let’s work it through Keep orange first. Both volumes are in litres, so the ratio is 1/2 : 5/8. Let’s count equal eighth-litre portions instead.  DO NOT PRINT — Figure reference V04. The following caption sentence is reader-facing. Use the same eighth-litre portion for both juices. Production note V04 — Solution model: two equal one-litre bars split into eight equal parts. Upper orange-juice bar has four orange cells and label 1/2 L = 4/8 L. Lower apple-juice bar has five teal cells and label 5/8 L. Label each cell 1/8 L. Unfilled cells remain white. Names and counts must work without colour. Use the current ChandRush colour and design style wherever it supports understanding. If that style would reduce clarity, retain the teaching colours, contrast and mathematical design required here. Mathematical accuracy and comprehension take priority. The orange juice fills 4 portions; the apple juice fills 5. Here is how multiplying both fractions by 8 counts those portions: 1/2 × 8 = (1 × 8)/2 = 8 ÷ 2 = 4 5/8 × 8 = (5 × 8)/8 = 40 ÷ 8 = 5 orange juice : apple juice = 4 : 5 The drawing also shows that the orange volume is 4/5 of the apple volume. The apple volume is 5/4 of the orange volume. Keep track of which juice you are comparing with which. END PANEL CH03-PAGE010-PANEL01 |
