END NON-PRINTING CARRIED PRODUCTION CONTEXT ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT Ratios Involving Fractions and Decimals The numbers in a ratio are called its terms. In simplest form, the terms are whole numbers with no common factor greater than 1. A common factor is a whole number that divides both terms exactly. For example, 2 : 3 is in simplest form. We cannot divide both 2 and 3 by the same whole number greater than 1 and still get whole numbers. What about 2.5 : 3? Think of two lengths measured in the same unit. Count them in half-unit pieces: 2.5 has 5 halves and 3 has 6 halves. The comparison becomes 5 : 6. In numbers: 2.5 : 3 = (2.5 × 2) : (3 × 2) = 5 : 6 We multiplied both terms by 2. We changed the size of the pieces we counted, while keeping the comparison the same. We’ll explore that idea further in Section 3.2. END PANEL CH03-PAGE008-PANEL02 |
