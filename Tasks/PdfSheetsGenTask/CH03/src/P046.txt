END NON-PRINTING CARRIED PRODUCTION CONTEXT ORIGINAL PANEL CONTENT Performance Task Golden Ratio Some comparisons have special names. Two amounts are in the golden ratio when the larger divided by the smaller is approximately 1.618. We write larger : smaller ≈ 1.618 : 1. You may see claims that buildings at the Acropolis in Athens were designed using this ratio. Let’s check the evidence before treating that as a fact. Drawing a rectangle over a photograph does not prove that the builder planned to use this ratio. 1. Draw a rectangle whose longer side and shorter side follow the golden ratio. Choose a sensible shorter side, then work out a longer side about 1.618 times as long. Try your drawing before looking at mine. One possible drawing Choose a shorter side of 5 cm. The longer side should be about 5 × 1.618 = 8.09 cm. An ordinary millimetre ruler lets us mark about 8.1 cm, so our hand-drawn rectangle will be an approximation. Use 1 mm grid paper. Draw a horizontal line 8.1 cm long, starting your ruler at its 0 mark. From each end, follow the paper’s vertical grid lines up 5 cm. Join the two top ends. The grid helps keep all four corners square.  DO NOT PRINT — Figure reference V18. The following caption sentence is reader-facing.
