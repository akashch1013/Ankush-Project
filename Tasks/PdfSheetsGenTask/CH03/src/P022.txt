Let’s work it through The ratio 6 : 4 tells us how to share the sweets. It does not say Tony gets only 6 sweets and Rudy only 4. Think of 6 equal parts for Tony and 4 equal parts for Rudy.  DO NOT PRINT — Figure reference V11. The following caption sentence is reader-facing. The 20 sweets fill 10 equal parts. Production note V11 — Solution bar model: exactly ten equal cells; first six teal labelled Tony, last four gold labelled Rudy. Brace the complete bar as 20 sweets. Each cell contains two sweet symbols; total count exactly 20. Group labels six parts and four parts distinguish ratio terms from actual sweet counts. Use the current ChandRush colour and design style wherever it supports understanding. If that style would reduce clarity, retain the teaching colours, contrast and mathematical design required here. Mathematical accuracy and comprehension take priority. Total parts = 6 + 4 = 10 Sweets in 1 part = 20 ÷ 10 = 2 Now multiply each number of parts by 2: Tony: 6 × 2 = 12 sweets;     Rudy: 4 × 2 = 8 sweets Check both conditions: 12 + 8 = 20 sweets, and 12 : 8 = (12 ÷ 2) : (8 ÷ 2) = 6 : 4. The total and the sharing rule both fit. END PANEL CH03-PAGE022-PANEL01 |
